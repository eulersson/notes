## Life of the Buddha

Don't start about about the miracles and great achievements. Instead start from his humanness.

1. Did prince Siddhartha really stop the (painful) tears?
2. If yes, how did he succeed at this?

Example: *Many people have **tinnitus**! They want to know about each other's stories and methods of improvements.* This is **wisdom** — to know where to look for: experience, and knowing they really speak from experience.

Prince Siddhartha could see injustice, nasty conflicts. Not about religion, it's about reality. Kingdom fights, wars. Then he thought further than his own kingdom, “How can I help people” (1. **motivation**).  Staying in the palace was not an option, since even within it there was also injustice that couldn't be completely mitigated.

He didn't know the way, so he searched teachers. The first was acharya **Alara Kakama**. He reached the same level of realization as the teacher. Siddhartha was very confident with his level of meditation. The student Siddhartha therefore was guided to another teacher,  **Udraka Ramaputra**. He also achieved his level of realization. 

“The problem of all beings are still there, I need to find another solution”

**6 years following an ascetic way.** After that, decided the **middle way** (no ascetism; no luxury) is most convenient. The wisdom of emptiness is realized in the **middle way**.

## Single-Pointed Meditation

About single-pointed meditation:

1. **Body posture** ([[Buddhism 7 Point Posture of Vairochana]])
2. **Focal point**
	1. Breathe in, breath out.
	2. 21 cycles (or 5 minutes ≈ 60 cycles)
	3. If lost count, restart from 0. 
	4. Multi task it with tiny white dot visualization between nose and upper lip, 2-3-4mm diameter.
3. Identifying errors of meditations
	1. Mental laxity and excitement.
4. Applying remedies to overcome the errors
	1. **Introspection**: keep an eye on the mind to see what it is doing. Vigilance. Spying on your mind. Once it notices big changes it hands the task to *Mindfulness* (see below).
	2. **Mindfulness**: the rope. Tying it to the pillar.
	3. Both remedies help help increase *reactivity* but does not help with *productivity*.

## Teachings and Schools

The Buddha needed an assistant and they appointed Ananda — his first cousin to be. Ananda accepted under the condition to only give teachings in his presence There's said to be 84.000 teachings from the Buddha — for the 84.000 mental defilements. Some sources mention 80.000. This is because before Ananda became the assistant the Buddha already taught 4.000 *(therefore some sources might start counting from the point where Ananda was witnessing them?)*

All the teachings (for each affliction) can be grouped into 3 families — 3 turnings of the wheel of Dharma — **attachment**, **anger** and **ignorance**.

Wee need to consider:
- Place where the teaching was done
- Subject matter
- Target audience\*
<small>* we need to know the schools at that time</small>

## 3 Natures

Anything that comes in mind must fall into one of two categories:

- Non-Existent
- Existent
	- Impermanent
	- Permanent
		- Ultimate Reality
		- Permanent other than ultimate reality


From those elements “Which 4 items cannot be divided any further?” — non-existence, impermanence, permanent reality and permanent phenomena other than permanent reality (those are the leaves of the nested list).

On the other side there's the *3 natures*: **imputed nature**,  **other-powered nature** / **dependant nature**,  **thoroughly established nature**.

Now we must match the two lists:


| Anything that comes to mind             | Three Natures                 |
| --------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Impermanent                             | Other Powered Nature          |
| Ultimate Reality                        | Thoroughly Established Nature |
| Non-Existent                            | Imputed Nature                |
| Impermanent Other than Ultimate Reality | Imputed Nature                |

During the **first** Turning (four noble truths exists truly) he meant *Other-powered phenomena and thoroughly established nature exists truly* and in the **second** (nothing exists truly) *Imputed nature does not existing truly*
