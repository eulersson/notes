> [!NOTE]- How to read the gloss
> Each Tibetan line is followed by a word-by-word gloss, in the Tibetan word order, and then by an English translation.
> The glosses are my own study aid, not revised by a teacher, so they may contain mistakes.
>
> **Labels**
>
> | Label | Meaning | Tibetan |
> | --- | --- | --- |
> | GEN | genitive, “of” | ཀྱི་ གྱི་ གི་ འི་ ཡི་ |
> | INS | instrumental: the doer or the means, “by, through” | ཀྱིས་ གྱིས་ གིས་ ས་ ཡིས་ |
> | OBL | oblique, the ལ་དོན་ particles: “to, in, on, as” | ལ་ ར་ རུ་ སུ་ ཏུ་ དུ་ |
> | LOC | locative, “in, at” | ན་ after a noun |
> | ABL | ablative, “from, than” | ནས་ ལས་ after a noun |
> | PL | plural | རྣམས་ དག་ ཚོ་ |
> | TOP | topic | ནི་ |
> | EMPH | emphasis | ཉིད་ |
> | QUOT | quotation or naming, “thus, so called” | ཞེས་ ཅེས་ ཤེས་ |
> | NOM | nominaliser, turning a verb into a noun or adjective | པ་ བ་ མ་ |
> | AGT | agent, “one who” | མཁན་ |
> | CON | connective: “and, with” between nouns; after a verb, “and then, having” | དང་; ནས་ ལ་ ཀྱི་ after a verb |
> | CONT | continuative, linking clauses: “and, while” | ཅིང་ ཞིང་ ཤིང་ ཏེ་ སྟེ་ དེ་ |
> | COND | conditional, “if, when”; COND.even is “even if” | ན་ after a verb; ན་ཡང་ ནའང་ |
> | NEG | negation; NEG.exist is “there is not”, NEG.have is “without” | མ་ མི་; མེད་ |
> | OPT | optative, “may…” | ཤོག་; ཅིག་ ཞིག་ ཤིག་ after གྱུར་ |
> | IMP | imperative | ཤིག་ after a verb, or .IMP on an imperative stem |
> | PST | past stem | .PST on the verb |
> | PROG | progressive, “-ing” | བཞིན་པ་ |
> | FIN | final particle, closing a sentence (TERM in some glosses) | འོ་ and its forms: གོ་ ངོ་ དོ་ ནོ་ བོ་ མོ་ རོ་ ལོ་ སོ་ ཏོ་ |
> | HON | honorific word | སྐུ་ ཐུགས་ ཕྱག་ གཤེགས་ གཟིགས་ … |
> | HUM | humble word, for one's own actions; a lone HUM in a mantra is the syllable hūṃ | བགྱི་ མཆི་ |
> | 1sg, 2sg | first and second person singular; 2sg.HON is the honorific “you” | བདག་ ང་; ཁྱོད་; ཁྱེད་ |
>
> **Symbols**
>
> - `.` joins a particle to the word it is fused with: བློས་ is `mind.INS`, and པའི་ is `NOM.GEN`.
> - `-` joins the English for a single Tibetan word or name: `sentient-being` (སེམས་ཅན་), `Jigme-Lingpa`.
> - `*` means careful: the word has other meanings elsewhere, or the reading is uncertain.
>   The note's Notes section explains it.
> - `?` marks a word I could not gloss with confidence.
> - `/` separates alternative meanings: `possess/having`.
> - `[ ]` adds what the Tibetan leaves out: `Garab[-Dorje]`.
> - `(Skt.)` marks a Sanskrit mantra, transliterated rather than glossed, with its seed syllables in capitals (HUM, HRIH).
