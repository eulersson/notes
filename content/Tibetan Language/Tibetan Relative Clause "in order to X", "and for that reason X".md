In [[Tibetan Relative Clauses]] it shows how to make relative clauses expressing *that* or *which* for <i class="p">direct objects</i> with <i class="y">པའི་</i> (པ་ + *connective particle* འབྲེལ་སྒྲ་), e.g. (the milk **that** I have to the cat).

There are two relative clause constructions that can work in pair, one expressing the same as the other but in reverse logical direction: 

| <i class="g">པར་</i>     | <i class="b">པས</i>་  |
| ------------------------ | --------------------- |
| པ་ལ་                     | པ་ + བྱེད་སྒྲ་        |
| *in order to X*, *for X* | *and for that reason* |

## Examples

བོད་ཡིག་སློབ་<i class="g">པར་</i>ང་ལྷ་སར་སོང་།
*I went to Lhasa <i class="g">in order to</i> study Tibetan.*

བོད་ཡིག་བསླབས་<i class="b">པས་</i>ང་ལྷ་སར་སོང་།
*I studied Tibetan, <i class="b">and for that reason</i> I went to Lhasa.*

## Contradictions

#help However there are cases where this hypothesis does not work:

དཔེ་ཆ་རྙེད་<i class="g">པར་</i>ང་ཚོང་ཁང་དུ་ཕྱིན།
I went to the store <i class="g">in order to</i> find a book.

😵‍💫 དཔེ་ཆ་རྙེད་<i class="b">པས་</i>ང་ཚོང་ཁང་དུ་ཕྱིན།
😵‍💫 I found a book, <i class="b">and for that reason</i> I went to the store.
