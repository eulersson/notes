There's a homogenous style in what concers construction in Mustangi towns.

![[Walking Around Lo Manthang.jpg|Walking Around Lo manthang]]

In the previous section on [[Mustang Mineral Pigments]] I showed the color palette seen on stupas, monasteries. Regular houses are covered in a white wash that reminded me of [[Santorini]], but the purpose is different. In Santorini is to reflect the light, keep the interior cooler and to prevent infections; in mustang is to protect from the erosion of the winds, since mud bricks would be easily damaged otherwise.

![[Mustang Streets.mp4]]

Homes have an **smokeless metal stoves** (*chulho* in Nepali) in the middle of the dining / living room (serves both purposes). By burning **yak dung**, heat is produced to cook and to keep the space warm. They don't use wood at the floors as I saw in [[Solukhumbu]] but instead they have a sort of pressed packed mud. Wood is scarce in Mustang as opposed to [[Solukhumbu]].

![[Mustang Smokeless Metal Stove.mp4]]

Another iconic aspect of Mustangi homes: wood is piles on the rooftops. They serve as burn stock and insulation.
